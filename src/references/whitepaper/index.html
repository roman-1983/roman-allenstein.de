Agentic CommerceWhat autonomous AI agents change about digital commerce

The argument

Search and browse is being replaced by delegate and execute. The customer does not disappear, but steps back: research, comparison and increasingly the checkout itself are handed to an agent, and the machine becomes the party that decides. That single move breaks the assumption every storefront of the last twenty years was built on - that a human is looking at it.

The consequence is uncomfortable for most organisations. Visibility stops coming from rankings and ad budgets and starts coming from machine-readable product data and open interfaces. A shop that an agent cannot find, parse or trust is not ranked lower - it is skipped. And unlike previous platform shifts, the infrastructure is being laid right now, by parties large enough to make their choices stick.

What it covers

  • The paradigm shift The delegated decision loop as the new customer journey, and the three interaction models it produces.
  • Market and players Where the forecasts from the large analyst houses agree and where they do not, who the shopping agents are, and why B2B is the bigger field.
  • The protocol landscape The agentic commerce stack: MCP as the foundation, agent-to-agent communication, the payment and identity protocols the card networks have launched, and UCP as the converging standard.
  • From SEO to AEO Why classical search visibility is no longer sufficient, what answer-engine optimisation demands of product data, and how exposure differs by product category.
  • Agent readiness Data quality, technical infrastructure, a touchpoint checklist and an assessment along an automation curve, so the abstract question becomes a score.
  • Shopware and Shopify Where both platforms stand on agentic commerce, their strengths and gaps, and concrete measures for customers on each.
  • Regulation and governance The European framework, and the case that regulation is an opportunity rather than only a brake.
  • Outlook A timeline, the limitations and open questions, three fields of action, and a glossary for the terms the field is still arguing about.

Beyond the document

The whitepaper is the centre of a set rather than a standalone paper. A two-page management summary carries the argument to people who will not read fifty pages. Two implementation concepts translate the recommendations into concrete steps - one for Shopware, one for Shopify - from structured data and MCP endpoints through to agent monitoring, each backed by working example code. A team session and a client webinar take the same material to two very different audiences.

Writing it was as much an editorial problem as a technical one. The field produces announcements faster than evidence, so a good part of the work was separating what is shipping from what is a press release, and marking the difference honestly - including a chapter on limitations, which is the part that ages best.
